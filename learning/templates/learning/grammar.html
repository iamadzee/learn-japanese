Core Japanese Grammar

Understand how Japanese sentences fit together using SOV order and relational particles.

1. Sentence Structure & The Copula (です)

Unlike English (Subject-Verb-Object), Japanese is an SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) language. The verb or state always comes at the end of the clause.

[Topic/Subject] + [Particle] + [Noun/Adjective] + です (desu)
わたしは学生です。
Watashi wa gakusei desu.
I am a student.
今日は暑いです。
Kyou wa atsui desu.
Today is hot.

2. Fundamental Particles (助詞)

Particles follow words to indicate their grammatical role in the sentence.

は (wa) Topic Marker: Identifies what the sentence is about.

田中たなかさんは先生です。
Tanaka-san wa sensei desu.
As for Tanaka, he is a teacher.

を (o / wo) Direct Object Marker: Marks the entity receiving the verb's action.

水を飲みます。
Mizu o nomimasu.
I drink water.

に (ni) Target / Time / Direction Marker: Pinpoints a specific destination or point in time.

七時に起きます。
Shichi-ji ni okimasu.
I wake up at 7 o'clock.

で (de) Location of Action / Means: Marks where an active event takes place, or the tool used.

図書館で勉強します。
Toshokan de benkyou shimasu.
I study at the library.

3. Polite Verb Endings (〜ます)

In polite conversation (Keigo / Teineigo), non-past affirmative verbs end in 〜ます (masu), and negatives end in 〜ません (masen).

Verb Stem + ます (Affirmative) / ません (Negative)
本を読みます。
Hon o yomimasu.
I read a book. / I will read a book.
肉を食べません。
Niku o tabemasen.
I do not eat meat.
Grammar tip

Japanese is usually written without spaces. Particles such as は, を, に, and で still show how each word relates to the verb.
